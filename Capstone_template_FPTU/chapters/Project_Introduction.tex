\subsection{Project Overview}
In contemporary digital communication, text input on mobile touchscreens has become an indispensable aspect of daily productivity and social interaction. However, typing Vietnamese on compact virtual keyboards remains inherently challenging due to geometric constraints, complex tonal diacritics, and rapid typing habits. Consequently, users frequently adopt unaccented shorthand, omit inter-word whitespace, make keyboard-adjacent typing mistakes, leave residual \ac{IME} composition codes, and employ informal abbreviations.

The \textbf{NextKey} project proposes an open, research-driven framework for end-to-end Vietnamese compact writing restoration. Rather than treating diacritic restoration, word segmentation, and typographical error correction as separate, disconnected tasks, NextKey formulates the problem as a unified sequence transformation task optimized for sub-millisecond edge execution.

\section{Project Background}

\subsection{Overview of the Field}
Vietnamese is an Austroasiatic, tonal language that utilizes the Latin-based \textit{Quoc Ngu} writing system. The orthography incorporates a sophisticated system of diacritical marks: 6 tone registers (unaccented, acute, grave, hook above, tilde, and dot below) combined with phonetic vowel and consonant modifications. The omission of diacritics introduces pervasive lexical ambiguity; for instance, the unaccented syllable \textit{"ca"} may correspond to multiple distinct words such as \textit{"ca"} (song/case), \textit{"c\'a"} (fish), \textit{"c\`a"} (eggplant), or \textit{"c\textasciicaron{a}"} (all).

In mobile computing, text entry primarily relies on rule-based composition standards such as Telex and VNI. Under time-constrained or casual typing scenarios, users frequently type phonetically without completing accent key combinations, concatenate words to minimize keystrokes, and touch adjacent virtual keys inadvertently on standard \ac{QWERTY} layouts. Resolving these compound noisy inputs requires a synthesis of \ac{NLP}, multi-task sequence modeling, and efficient on-device edge computing.

\subsection{Existing Systems and Related Work}
Contemporary literature and industrial solutions can be categorized into four primary paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rule-Based Input Method Editors (IMEs):} Standard software engines such as Unikey, OpenKey, and EVKey depend strictly on deterministic keystroke composition tables. They operate at the character-entry level without contextual awareness and cannot retroactively reconstruct unaccented or typo-corrupted sentences.
    \item \textbf{Commercial Smart Keyboards:} Advanced mobile keyboards such as Google Gboard, Microsoft SwiftKey, and Laban Key incorporate local n-gram language models and statistical candidate prediction. However, their corrective mechanisms operate predominantly on sliding local windows and struggle when confronted with continuous, unsegmented, multi-error streams.
    \item \textbf{Statistical and Classical Diacritic Restoration Models:} Traditional research efforts have utilized n-gram language models, Hidden Markov Models (HMM), and \ac{CRF} \cite{Pham2021}. These models universally presuppose that input text is already accurately tokenized into distinct words and completely devoid of spelling errors, rendering them fragile in realistic mobile environments.
    \item \textbf{Pretrained Transformer and Large Language Models:} Recent approaches employ deep neural encoders and autoregressive decoders such as PhoBERT \cite{Nguyen2019}, ViT5, and multilingual text-to-text models (mT5, ByT5) \cite{Vaswani2017}. While these architectures demonstrate strong contextual representation, their immense parameter footprint ($> 100\text{M}$ parameters) and high inference latency ($> 300\text{ ms}$) prevent direct, offline execution on resource-constrained mobile client hardware.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations of Existing Solutions}
A critical review of the literature reveals three fundamental technological and methodological gaps:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fragmented Pipeline Assumption:} Existing systems isolate diacritic recovery, word boundary segmentation, and spell correction into sequential stages. Consequently, an error generated in an upstream module (e.g., erroneous word boundary detection) cascades uncontrollably into subsequent accentuation stages.
    \item \textbf{Unrealistic Noise Modeling:} Most benchmark datasets are artificially generated by merely stripping diacritics from clean corpora. They fail to capture the compound noise distributions encountered in real-world mobile input, including geometric keyboard-neighbor typos, Telex composition residue, and abbreviation colloquialisms.
    \item \textbf{Edge Execution and Privacy Infeasibility:} Cloud-reliant neural systems introduce network transmission latency, depend on continuous internet availability, and pose severe privacy vulnerabilities regarding the exfiltration of sensitive user keystroke data.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Statement}

Let an observed, corrupted input character sequence of length $T$ be represented as:
\begin{equation}
    X = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_T), \quad x_t \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{input}}
\end{equation}
where $X$ may concurrently exhibit:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Complete absence of Vietnamese tonal markers and vowel/consonant modifications.
    \item Partial or total omission of inter-word whitespace boundaries.
    \item Typographical errors resulting from spatial key proximity on virtual \ac{QWERTY} keyboards (e.g., unintended substitution of adjacent keys).
    \item Phonetic abbreviations and residual multi-stroke typing sequences (e.g., Telex keystroke artifacts).
\end{enumerate}

The overarching research goal of \textbf{NextKey} is to learn a robust, parameterized mapping function $f_\theta: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ that accurately reconstructs the clean, canonical Vietnamese sentence:
\begin{equation}
    Y = (y_1, y_2, \dots, y_M), \quad y_m \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{output}}
\end{equation}
while satisfying strict operational boundaries for client-side edge deployment: peak inference latency $\tau < 1.0\text{ ms}$ on standard mobile \ac{CPU} architectures and an overall model storage footprint $\mathcal{M} < 2.0\text{ MB}$.

\section{Research Questions}

The theoretical and empirical investigation of NextKey is structured around six core research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Restoration Feasibility):} Can a unified machine learning system accurately restore standard Vietnamese text from compact input corrupted simultaneously by missing diacritics, merged spacing, abbreviations, and keyboard-adjacent typos?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Architectural Suitability):} Which model family (recurrent sequence taggers, temporal convolutional networks, lightweight transformer encoders, or sequence-to-sequence decoders) offers the optimal Pareto trade-off between restoration quality and edge efficiency?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Noise Modeling Impact):} Does the incorporation of keyboard-aware synthetic noise distributions significantly improve restoration robustness on human-written compact text compared to conventional accent-stripping noise models?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Edge Compression Viability):} Can ultra-compact student models preserve high linguistic fidelity after Knowledge Distillation (\ac{KD}) and \ac{INT8} quantization?
    \item \textbf{RQ5 (Candidate Generation Utility):} Does a multi-candidate (Top-$k$) generation and reranking mechanism enhance user-perceived utility in interactive typing scenarios?
    \item \textbf{RQ6 (Privacy and Adaptation):} Can lightweight, on-device personalization mechanisms adapt to user-specific abbreviations while upholding strict privacy constraints?
\end{itemize}

\section{Project Objectives}

To systematically address the research questions, the project defines the following specific, measurable, and achievable objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dataset and Taxonomy Formalization:} Construct the \textbf{JDWR v1} multi-domain dataset ($> 840,000$ sentences across 8 distinct news domains) and formalize a realistic 6-dimensional typing noise taxonomy modeling virtual \ac{QWERTY} keyboard geometry.
    \item \textbf{Open Architectural Benchmarking:} Formulate an open model-selection protocol to systematically evaluate candidate model families (including recurrent \ac{BiGRU}/\ac{BiLSTM}, convolutional \ac{TCN}, and attention-based architectures) under identical data splits and evaluation constraints.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Task Conditioning Exploration:} Investigate hierarchical multi-task neural architectures capable of jointly predicting character corrections, word segmentation boundaries, and diacritic tags without inter-task error propagation.
    \item \textbf{Edge-First Optimization Framework:} Establish model compression and distillation pipelines to reduce memory footprint by $> 70\%$ and ensure sub-millisecond per-sentence inference on consumer-grade mobile \ac{CPU} hardware.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Tier Evaluation and Delivery:} Implement a standardized 3-tier evaluation harness (spanning Character, Word, and Sentence-level metrics) and deliver reproducible demonstration interfaces (\ac{REST} \ac{API} and interactive web \ac{UI}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Significance of the Project}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Theoretical and Scientific Significance:} NextKey advances the state of Vietnamese \ac{NLP} by shifting the academic paradigm from narrow, isolated diacritic restoration to realistic, compound-noise compact writing restoration. It provides empirical insights into architectural scaling behaviors, topological trade-offs, and multi-task feature gating under character-level language constraints.
    \item \textbf{Practical and Technological Value:} The resulting ultra-compact models offer an immediately deployable, zero-latency inference engine suitable for integration into embedded mobile operating systems, virtual keyboards, and assistive accessibility tools.
    \item \textbf{User Privacy and Societal Impact:} By enabling complete on-device execution without transmitting keystroke streams to remote cloud servers, NextKey provides absolute data sovereignty and privacy protection for millions of Vietnamese digital device users.
\end{itemize}

\section{Project Scope \& Limitations}

\subsection{In-Scope}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Multi-domain Vietnamese linguistic data covering journalism, administration, everyday conversation, and general texts.
    \item Joint modeling of missing diacritics, word boundary concatenation, and spatial keyboard-adjacent typographical errors.
    \item Open empirical exploration across multiple neural sequence modeling families and compression techniques (\ac{KD}, \ac{PTQ}, \ac{QKD}).
    \item Standalone evaluation harnesses, modular \ac{REST} \ac{API} endpoints, and web-based interactive demonstration prototypes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out-of-Scope \& Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Acoustic speech-to-text processing or multimodal input modalities.
    \item Low-level kernel driver development for specific proprietary mobile operating systems (the project provides standard SDK/API interfaces for core inference).
    \item Highly specialized ancient historical scripts or rare ethnic minority dialectal forms beyond standard contemporary Vietnamese.
\end{itemize}
